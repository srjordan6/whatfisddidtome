I asked Frisco ISD how children move up in its swim program.

It had no published answer. Here is what happened after I asked, in order, with dates, from the District's own documents.

In short

It started with a question. On February 2, 2026 I asked my son's coaches whether anyone in his training group had been moved up to Gold. Both answered in writing. One wrote that there had been no move-ups and that they would come in March. The other wrote, "To be clear nobody has moved up." I then learned that another swimmer from his group was already practicing with Gold.

I believed I had been told something untrue, and I put it in writing. On February 25 I filed a grievance asking that my son be moved to Gold. On February 26 I filed a report with the District's ethics hotline about the coaches' statements and a possible conflict of interest. When I asked what times a swimmer needed to reach Gold, I was given the time ranges of the boys already in the group, not a published standard. The program's own group requirements say Gold is by coach invitation only.

Iron Horse Aquatics is a competitive swim program that Frisco ISD runs at its own natatorium with District employees. On March 25 my son was moved to Gold. That same day I asked for written advancement criteria that every family could see. Two days later the District's Swim and Dive Coordinator wrote that if I continued to be dissatisfied, she could give me contact information for other clubs.

I kept asking for published criteria. In May the District told me in writing that the Board of Trustees would not hear it. On June 9 the program's head coach opened a Parent Code of Conduct investigation into me about two hours before I filed an appeal with the Texas Education Agency. On July 7, the day after the U.S. Center for SafeSport declined to take the matter, I was barred from every Iron Horse event through December 31, 2026, under threat of criminal trespass. I have never been told who accused me or what they said.

My son left Iron Horse because I could no longer attend. The program took no action against him.

The program belongs to the District

This matters because the District has taken both positions on it.

In its July 15, 2026 answer to my state appeal, the District's outside counsel wrote that Iron Horse is not within the Board of Trustees' oversight authority. Fifteen days later, in a July 30 filing in the same agency, the same counsel wrote that the District owns and manages Iron Horse Aquatics.

The District's own budgets settle it. Every year from 2022–23 through 2026–27, the District's published line item budget carries Iron Horse in its General Fund, in fund 164 and organization code 912, labeled "Iron Horse (Swim Club)." Iron Horse coaching salaries are charged to District payroll. The 2022–23 budget, the last year the District published revenue detail, shows a revenue line titled "REVENUE/SWIM TEAM" budgeted at $670,000. Families pay their fees to the District.

The District codes its high school swim teams as function 36, Cocurricular and Extracurricular. It codes Iron Horse as function 61, Community Services. On June 15, 2026 the Board of Trustees adopted the 2026–27 budget at the fund and function level, including a General Fund appropriation of $2,453,059 for Community Services. Iron Horse is paid for from that appropriation.

The question

February 2, 2026

I asked my son's coaches whether anyone in his training group had been moved up to Gold. Both answered in writing. One wrote, "There have been no move-ups at this time," and that they were still looking to do move-ups in March. The other wrote, "To be clear nobody has moved up."

I then learned that another swimmer from my son's group was already practicing with Gold. In my February 24 email I described it as I understood it at the time: that the swimmer was still officially in Silver but was being allowed to practice with Gold. I asked that my son be given the same opportunity, or a formal evaluation for Gold.

February 24 and 25, 2026

I wrote to the coach and asked for the specific performance benchmarks required for Gold placement and whether my son was close to meeting them. He replied the next day with the current time ranges of the boys already in Gold in three freestyle events, and said my son was below them in freestyle. Those ranges describe the swimmers already in a group. They are not a standard a swimmer can aim for.

The program's group requirements, posted as a slide presentation linked from its website, say Gold is by "Invitation by Head Age Group Coach ONLY." They list no time standards for moving up.

February 25 and 26, 2026

I filed a formal grievance asking that my son be moved to Gold, and raising my concern that families were being told different things. The next day I followed up to the District's Athletics leadership and the Board of Trustees. That night I filed a report through the District's ethics hotline, Report 9022620263313, about the coaches' written statements and a possible conflict of interest. On May 21 the District told the hotline service that my report would be handled through the grievance process. It has never received a written disposition.

March 2 to 6, 2026

The District assigned my grievance at Level I to the Swim and Dive Coordinator. I objected in writing because she was one of the people my grievance was about. The District's General Counsel replied that the process starts at Level I with the assigned hearing officer. I attended the Level I conference on March 6 under protest. I asked for two things: my son's promotion, and transparency in how swimmers advance.

March 24 and 25, 2026

The Level I response issued on March 24. On March 25 my son was moved to Gold. That same day I asked in writing for the program's advancement criteria to be written down and made available to every family. That has been my request about the program ever since.

The answer

March 27, 2026, 7:06 PM

The District's Swim and Dive Coordinator replied, with the District's Complaint Resolution office copied:

Please reach out to your son's coach moving forward. If you continue to be dissatisfied, we can provide you with contact information for other clubs in the area that may be better suited to your needs.

Email from the District's Swim and Dive Coordinator, March 27, 2026

An hour later I filed an appeal asking for objective advancement benchmarks, a definition of "invitation only," and written confirmation that the criteria apply to every swimmer. I wrote that the appeal was not about my son's placement, which had been resolved.

The door closes

March 30 to April 27, 2026

The Complaint Resolution office treated my request for criteria as a new remedy and sent it back to Level I, to the same Swim and Dive Coordinator. I objected and then attended a second Level I conference on April 7. Her written response on April 27 denied the request, relying on coaching experience, differences in standards, and program capacity. I appealed to Level II.

May 5 and 18, 2026

The Level II conference was held May 5 at the District's Athletic Offices. On May 18 the Associate Director of Athletics denied the appeal. She found my claim that advancement was applied inconsistently unsubstantiated, explained that a state law requiring course syllabi does not apply to extracurricular activities, and wrote that districts are not required to publish advancement criteria between internal training groups. I had not asked whether the District was required to. I had asked it to.

May 18 to 21, 2026

I asked for a Level III appeal to the Board that day. The District told me to file it through its online complaint portal. On May 19 the portal would not let me enter anything; the fields were greyed out. It would fail again during my appeal windows on July 20 and 21 and on August 18.

On May 20 the District's Complaint Resolution Coordinator wrote that my complaint concerned an extracurricular activity, that a Level III complaint "will be dismissed," and that the Level II response was final. On May 21 the District's General Counsel wrote that complaints about extracurricular participation are not heard by the Board, and that the process "stops at Level II." So I went to the State.

The meet in Arkansas

Saturday, June 6, 2026

My son swam at the NWAA Memorial Classic, a USA Swimming meet in Bentonville, Arkansas. The District's decisions date it June 5. In the morning he finished second in the 50 meter breaststroke preliminary and qualified to swim again in the afternoon, in lane 5.

In the afternoon, when he stepped up to his starting block, another swimmer was in his place. He went to an official, who did nothing. I went to the entrance of the pool deck and asked the volunteer at the gate if I could speak with an official. She went and spoke with them. No official ever came to speak with me. I never spoke with, argued with, or threatened any official.

Meanwhile the officials put my son in the next heat, in lane 1. He was upset and finished sixth. I did not know the race had happened. He came over to me afterward. I was at the deck entrance, about 5 to 10 feet inside it. I told him not to just accept it and to speak up for himself.

Then I wrote to his coach in the team chat:

Total screw up with my boy today. I want to speak to who is running the meet. The only fair thing to do is run heat 2 of the 50 meter breast again.

My message to the coach

The coach replied that the entry error was an honest mistake, that the meet director and head official had found him a lane as a courtesy, and that he needed to address my conduct during the situation. I wrote back that the race should be swum again. The Swim and Dive Coordinator then wrote that this was not how it works, that at meets of this level he would usually not get the chance to compete at all, and that officials have the right to remove parents from a meet. I asked for a meeting the next morning with the coach and the meet director. She replied that a meeting would not be necessary.

I am going to let this drop this time, but coaches need to make certain that their swimmers are in the right event, heat and lane. You have to double check young kids.

My reply

The coach answered that it was an accident and an isolated incident. That was the end of it.

The District later found that I was argumentative toward meet volunteers and verbally threatening toward a meet official, based on a report it says came from staff at the Arkansas venue. The only volunteer I spoke to was the one at the gate, and I asked her only to let me speak to an official. I never did.

Two dates

4:35 PM
June 9, 2026. The head coach emails me to open a Parent Code of Conduct investigation.
6:32 PM
June 9, 2026. I file my petition with the Texas Education Agency.

About two hours apart.

July 6
The U.S. Center for SafeSport declines jurisdiction and refers the matter to USA Swimming.
July 7
A Summary of Investigation bars me from every Iron Horse event through December 31, 2026, and warns of criminal trespass.

One day apart.

The July 7 Summary of Investigation was issued under the names of four program staff: the Swim and Dive Coordinator, the head coach, and two coaches. The two coaches were the ones named in my February ethics report. The Swim and Dive Coordinator had written the March 27 email. The head coach had opened the investigation on June 9.

On June 15 I filed a retaliation grievance. On June 23 the Swim and Dive Coordinator wrote that the investigation was concluded. On July 7, after the suspension, I asked the Collin County district court for a temporary restraining order. I withdrew that case on July 11.

Texas Education Code Section 37.105 says who may refuse a person entry to or eject a person from district property: an administrator, a school resource officer, or a district peace officer. It requires a verbal warning, a record of that warning, and written information about how to appeal. On September 17 I asked the District in writing for those records. That letter is now part of the record before the Board. I have not received the records.

The appeals

July 20 and 21, 2026

The Associate Director of Athletics, the same administrator who had denied my criteria grievance at Level II, found my retaliation complaint "unsubstantiated." The finding applied the District's policy on discrimination and harassment, not its policy on parent grievances. I had declined her offer to meet because I had asked in writing that no one from the Athletics department review it. The portal returned an error when I tried to appeal, so I appealed by email.

August 12, 2026

The Level I decision denied all relief. It cited FERPA, the federal student privacy law, as the reason I could not learn who accused me. FERPA protects student education records. The people described as witnesses were adult volunteers and meet officials. If their statements concern only me, they are not education records. If they concern my son, I have a right as his parent to see them.

In August I wrote emails to a District administrator in a tone I regret. They are in the record.

August 24 to 26, 2026

The District designated its Managing Director of Athletics as the Level II hearing officer, the third reviewer in a row from the department my complaint was about. I objected in writing. At the August 26 hearing I asked who designated him and whether he had prior involvement. Neither question has been answered.

September 14, 2026

The Level II decision denied all relief. It said the investigation and consequences were based solely on my actions at the meet in Arkansas, and that as of March 25, 2026 the program had published its group requirements on its website. March 25 is the day I asked for the criteria. The group requirements still say Gold is by coach invitation only, and they refer to the 2026–2027 season.

September 2026

In May the District told me the Board would not hear my grievance about advancement criteria. In September it agreed the Board would hear my appeal on the retaliation grievance. On October 5 the District confirmed in writing that it is preserving all records related to me and that my supplemental filing was added to the Level III record.

October 19, 2026

The Board of Trustees considers my appeal. Under the Board's procedures, Level III appeals are decided on the written record unless two trustees request an oral presentation. The Board's written decision is due by November 18, 2026.

The State

Texas Education Agency

I have two appeals pending before the Commissioner of Education. Docket 120-R10-06-2026 concerns advancement criteria. Docket 159-R10-07-2026 concerns retaliation. On September 16 the District filed a 116 page local record in the retaliation docket. Every page carries the docket number of the other appeal, the record ends on August 5, and it does not include the Level II proceeding. I filed objections on September 18.

Texas Attorney General

I made three requests under the Public Information Act. In ruling on the first, the Attorney General accepted the District's representation that it reasonably anticipated litigation with me by June, and allowed most of the records to be withheld until that litigation ends. The second was withheld on the same ground. A ruling on the third, which asks for program contracts, accounts, and salaries, is pending.

What I got wrong

I went onto the pool deck, about 5 to 10 feet inside the entrance. I told my son not to accept what had happened to him. My first message to his coach was angry. I wrote emails in August in a tone I regret. I have described each of these plainly, because a reader should be able to judge the rest of this page knowing them.

What I am asking for

About the program: published, objective advancement criteria for Iron Horse Aquatics that apply to every family, applied consistently.

About what happened to me, in my appeal to the Board: that the decisions below be set aside, the July 7 suspension and its finding withdrawn, the witness statements and identities produced to me, and the matter reinvestigated by someone outside the Athletics department. I also asked the Board to find that the four employees named on the July 7 letter retaliated against me and to terminate their employment.

My son no longer swims for Iron Horse. Nothing I am asking for benefits him.
