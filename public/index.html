I asked Frisco ISD how children move up in its swim program.

It had no published answer. Here is what happened after I asked, in order, with dates, from the District's own documents.

In short

Iron Horse Aquatics is a competitive swim program that Frisco ISD runs at its own natatorium with its own employees. Children are placed in training groups, and moving up a group matters to them. When I asked what a swimmer had to do to move up, the program could not point to a written standard. Placement into its top group is by coach invitation only.

In February 2026 I filed an ethics report and a grievance asking for the standard. On March 25 I asked the District to publish objective advancement criteria for every family, not just mine. Two days later the District's Swim and Dive Coordinator told me that if I remained dissatisfied, she could give me the names of other clubs.

On June 9, the District opened a Parent Code of Conduct investigation into me about two hours before I filed an appeal with the Texas Education Agency. On July 7, the day after the U.S. Center for SafeSport declined to take the matter, four program staff barred me from every Iron Horse event through December 31, 2026, under threat of criminal trespass. I have never been told who accused me or what they said.

My son left Iron Horse because I could no longer attend. The program took no action against him.

The program belongs to the District

This matters because the District has taken both positions on it.

In its July 15, 2026 answer to my state appeal, the District's outside counsel wrote that Iron Horse is not within the Board of Trustees' oversight authority. Fifteen days later, in a July 30 filing in the same agency, the same counsel wrote that the District owns and manages Iron Horse Aquatics.

The District's own budgets settle it. Every year from 2022–23 through 2026–27, the District's published line item budget carries Iron Horse in its General Fund, in fund 164 and organization code 912, labeled "Iron Horse (Swim Club)." Iron Horse coaching salaries are charged to District payroll. The 2022–23 budget, the last year the District published revenue detail, shows a revenue line titled "REVENUE/SWIM TEAM" budgeted at $670,000. Families pay their fees to the District.

The District codes its high school swim teams as function 36, Cocurricular and Extracurricular. It codes Iron Horse as function 61, Community Services. On June 15, 2026 the Board of Trustees adopted the 2026–27 budget at the fund and function level, including a General Fund appropriation of $2,453,059 for Community Services. Iron Horse is paid for from that appropriation.

The question

February 2026

Two coaches told me there would be no move-ups at that time. I asked what benchmarks a swimmer needed to meet to advance. I was given group averages, which describe the swimmers already in a group, not a threshold a swimmer could aim for.

The program's own group requirements, posted as a slide presentation linked from its website, say the top age group is by "Invitation by Head Age Group Coach ONLY." They list no time standards for moving up. The age ranges of the groups overlap.

February 26, 2026

I filed a report through the District's ethics hotline, Report 9022620263313, and a formal grievance. The ethics report has never received a disposition.

March 25, 2026

My son's placement was resolved. That same day I asked the District to publish objective advancement criteria that would apply to every family in the program. That is the only thing I have asked for since.

The answer

March 27, 2026

The District's Swim and Dive Coordinator emailed me, with the District's Complaint Resolution office copied. This is the whole message:

Please reach out to your son's coach moving forward. If you continue to be dissatisfied, we can provide you with contact information for other clubs in the area that may be better suited to your needs.

Email from the District's Swim and Dive Coordinator, March 27, 2026

The door closes

May 18, 2026

My grievance was denied at Level II by the District's Associate Director of Athletics. The decision relied on a state statute about course syllabi. I had not asked for a syllabus. I had asked how a child moves from one group to the next.

May 19 and 20, 2026

The District's online complaint portal failed while my window to appeal was open. It would fail again on July 20 and 21 and on August 18, each time during an appeal window.

May 20 and 21, 2026

The Complaint Resolution office wrote that a Level III appeal to the Board "will be dismissed." The next day the District's General Counsel wrote that the process "stops at Level II." In writing, the District told me no one above the Athletics department would review my request. So I went to the State.

Bentonville

June 5, 2026

My son swam at an out of state meet in Bentonville, Arkansas. In the morning he finished second in his preliminary heat. In the afternoon, another swimmer took his position on the starting block. He went to an official, who did nothing, and he was placed in the next heat instead. He finished sixth.

I went to the gate that controls access to the pool deck and a few feet past it. I asked the volunteer at the gate if I could speak with the meet officials. She went over and spoke with them. No official ever came back to speak with me. I never spoke with, argued with, or threatened any official.

While I was there, I spoke to my son. I told him not to simply accept it and to speak up for himself.

Afterward I messaged his coach through the team chat and asked whether he could swim the event again. The Swim and Dive Coordinator replied that a meeting was not necessary. I wrote back that I would let it drop this time. The coach replied that it was an accident and an isolated incident. That ended the exchange.

The District later found that I was argumentative toward meet volunteers and verbally threatening toward a meet official. The only volunteer I spoke to was the one at the gate, and the only thing I asked her was to let me speak to an official. I never did.

Two dates

4:35 PM
June 9, 2026. The District opens a Parent Code of Conduct investigation into me.
6:32 PM
June 9, 2026. I file my petition with the Texas Education Agency.

About two hours apart.

July 6
The U.S. Center for SafeSport declines jurisdiction.
July 7
Four program staff bar me from every Iron Horse event through December 31, 2026, and warn of criminal trespass.

One day apart.

The four staff who signed the July 7 letter were the program's Swim and Dive Coordinator, its head coach, and two coaches. Two of them were the coaches named in my February ethics report. Another had written me the March 27 email.

Texas Education Code Section 37.105 says who may refuse a person entry to or eject a person from district property: an administrator, a school resource officer, or a district peace officer. It requires a verbal warning, a record of that warning, and written information about how to appeal. On September 17 I asked the District in writing for those records. They are now part of the record before the Board.

On June 15 I filed a retaliation grievance.

The appeals

July 20, 2026

The Associate Director of Athletics, the same administrator who had denied my first grievance at Level II, found my retaliation complaint "unsubstantiated." The finding applied the District's policy on discrimination and harassment, not its policy on parent grievances.

August 12, 2026

The Level I decision denied all relief. It cited FERPA, the federal student privacy law, as the reason I could not learn who accused me. FERPA protects student education records. The people described as witnesses were adult volunteers and meet officials. If their statements concern only me, they are not education records. If they concern my son, I have a right as his parent to see them.

In August I wrote emails to a District administrator in a tone I regret. They are in the record.

August 24 to 26, 2026

The District designated its Managing Director of Athletics as the Level II hearing officer, the third reviewer in a row from the department my complaint was about. I objected in writing. At the August 26 conference I asked who designated him and whether he had prior involvement. Neither question has been answered.

September 14, 2026

The Level II decision denied all relief. It said the consequences were based solely on the events of June 5, and that the program's criteria had been published as of March 25, 2026. The group requirements the program publishes still say the top group is by coach invitation only, and they refer to the 2026–2027 season.

September 2026

Having told me in May that the Board would never hear it, the District now told me the Board would hear my Level III appeal. On October 5 the District confirmed in writing that it is preserving all records related to me and that my supplemental filing was added to the Level III record.

October 19, 2026

The Board of Trustees considers my appeal. Under the Board's procedures, Level III appeals are decided on the written record unless two trustees request an oral presentation. The Board's written decision is due by November 18, 2026.

The State

Texas Education Agency

I have two appeals pending before the Commissioner of Education. Docket 120-R10-06-2026 concerns advancement criteria. Docket 159-R10-07-2026 concerns retaliation. On September 16 the District filed a 116 page local record in the retaliation docket. Every page carries the docket number of the other appeal, the record ends on August 5, and it does not include the Level II proceeding. I filed objections on September 18.

Texas Attorney General

I made three requests under the Public Information Act. In ruling on the first, the Attorney General accepted the District's representation that it reasonably anticipated litigation with me by June, and allowed most of the records to be withheld until that litigation ends. The second was withheld on the same ground. A ruling on the third, which asks for program contracts, accounts, and salaries, is pending.

What I got wrong

I went a few feet past the deck entrance in Bentonville. I told my son not to accept what had happened to him. I wrote emails in August in a tone I regret. I have tried to describe each of these plainly on this page, because a reader should be able to judge the rest of it knowing them.

What I am asking for

Published, objective advancement criteria for Iron Horse Aquatics that apply to every family in the program.

Withdrawal of the finding that I threatened a meet official, which I did not do.

That is all. My son no longer swims for Iron Horse. Nothing I am asking for benefits him.
